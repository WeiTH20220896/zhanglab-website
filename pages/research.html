Research

We investigate GPCR signaling across scales—from atomic structure to engineered receptors and new treatments for neurological disease.

Our research thesis

Understand the receptor.
Engineer the signal.
Advance the medicine.

GPCRs translate chemical information into cellular decisions. In the nervous system, that makes them both powerful biological switches and foundational drug targets.

Our lab links high-resolution structural biology with chemogenetic engineering and neuropharmacology. Each direction informs the others, creating a continuous path from mechanism to intervention.

Structural biology

See the mechanism

GPCR structural biology

We combine cryo-electron microscopy, molecular pharmacology, computational modeling, and molecular dynamics to resolve how ligands reshape receptor conformations and control downstream signaling.

  • Ligand recognition and receptor activation
  • Allosteric regulation and signaling bias
  • GPCR–transducer complex assembly
Chemogenetics

Rewrite the response

DREADD tool development

We use structure-guided receptor engineering to develop DREADDs with precise ligand selectivity, signaling profiles, and cellular control—building better tools for dissecting neural circuits and disease biology.

  • Structure-guided receptor design
  • Selective designer ligand discovery
  • Neural-circuit and cell-type control
Drug discovery

Move toward treatment

Neurological drug development

We translate GPCR mechanisms into therapeutic hypotheses, integrating virtual screening, pharmacological profiling, and functional assays to discover and optimize compounds for neurological disorders.

  • Structure-based virtual screening
  • Cellular pharmacology and selectivity profiling
  • Lead discovery for neurological disease
