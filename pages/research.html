Research Overview

Our lab focuses on the structural basis and functional regulation of G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs), building a systematic research framework centered on "Structural Elucidation — Receptor Engineering — Neurological Drug Discovery." GPCRs are one of the most important membrane protein signaling families in the human body, broadly involved in neurotransmitter regulation, sensory perception, and endocrine signaling, and represent the most critical class of drug targets in modern pharmaceutical research.

GPCR Structural Biology

Starting from high-resolution structures and conformational dynamics of GPCRs, we combine cryo-EM structural determination, computational modeling, and molecular dynamics simulations to dissect ligand-induced conformational changes, revealing the coupling mechanisms between orthosteric binding pockets, allosteric regulatory sites, and G protein signaling interfaces — providing a structural foundation for understanding receptor activation and signaling bias.

DREADD Tool Development

DREADDs (Designer Receptors Exclusively Activated by Designer Drugs) are engineered GPCRs generated through site-directed mutagenesis that lose responsiveness to endogenous ligands while gaining selective activation or inhibition by specific synthetic small molecules. This technology has become an essential tool for dissecting neural circuit function and chemogenetic control. Our lab advances the rational design of novel DREADD chemogenetic tools, using structure-guided engineering to construct receptors that are inactivated toward endogenous ligands yet highly selective for designer small molecules.

Neurological Drug Discovery

Building on structural mechanistic research and molecular pharmacology, our lab pursues GPCR-targeted small molecule drug screening and optimization for neurological diseases, with a focus on selective modulation of central receptors and biased signaling drug design. By integrating large-scale virtual screening, molecular dynamics simulations, AI-assisted screening, and in vitro functional validation, we aim to provide new molecular tools and research foundations for precision intervention in neuropsychiatric disorders.
